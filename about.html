The person doing the work

You'll always know who's been in your car.

When you book, you get me. More than a hundred cars now, all of them start to finish. Nobody to pass you along to, and one person to tell if something isn't right.

I started on cars nobody was paying me for. A teammate's back seat after a road trip, my mom's car after a summer of hauling, my own because the door pocket was driving me up the wall. People kept offering money and eventually I stopped saying no.

I play college basketball, so practice is early and away games take the weekend. Everything else gets built around them, which is how I end up in a driveway at seven in the morning or on a Tuesday when nobody else will come out.

What I won't skip is the part you can't see. Seams, rails, under the seats, the vents. That's where a smell lives, and the first thing to come back if someone hurried past it. If your car needs longer than we thought, I'd rather stay late.
