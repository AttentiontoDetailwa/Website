Attention to Detail

Six mobile homepages.

The desktop layout stays as it is. These are six ways the same homepage could behave on a phone — same photos, same prices, same voice, different structure. 06 is the one I'd build: it takes 02's drag-to-compare, keeps the prices vertical where you can compare them, and reserves swiping for the gallery. Scroll each frame below, or open one full screen on your own phone.

What's actually wrong today

  • Type is still at desktop scale — the headline lands at 42px on a 375px screen and eats three lines before anything else appears.
  • The homepage is 8,840px tall on a phone. That's about eleven full screens of scrolling to reach the footer.
  • Below 440px the wordmark disappears entirely — the header is a small car icon, a phone number and a menu button.
  • The before/after pair sits side by side at 3:4, so each photo is about 150px wide and lands below the fold anyway.
  • Pricing runs 2,164px and the footer another 1,344px — four stacked columns nobody scrolls to.
  • No persistent way to call or quote once you've scrolled past the hero.
06

Proof First

The synthesis, and the one I'd build. The drag-to-compare gets the hero slot because it's the only thing that proves the work in one gesture — running the red Audi pair from the desktop hero. Pricing is 05's segmented control with the figures in the tabs, so all three numbers stay on screen while the panel switches. Swiping is reserved for the gallery, where hiding the next photo costs nothing. Concept 01's scale and bottom rail underneath.

Thumb Rail

Your current design, rebuilt at phone scale. Type comes down, sections get shorter, pricing becomes three rows instead of three tall cards, and a fixed bar at the bottom always holds Call and Get a quote. The safe pick — nothing new to approve.

02

Swipe Deck

Anything that was a row of three on desktop swipes sideways instead of stacking. Pricing cards, the gallery and the proof strip all snap-scroll horizontally, and the hero carries a real drag-to-compare before/after. Roughly a third of the scroll depth.
